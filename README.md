# Vehicle Registration Trend Analysis

![Python](https://img.shields.io/badge/python-3.x-blue) ![scikit-learn](https://img.shields.io/badge/scikit--learn-ML-orange) ![status](https://img.shields.io/badge/data-synthetic-lightgrey)

A data analysis and machine learning mini project in Python. It looks at how new vehicle
registrations change over time, forecasts the next 12 months, and groups states that behave alike.

> **Important:** the dataset in this repo is **made up** (generated by `make_sample_data.py`).
> The results show how the method works. They are **not** facts about the real market.

## What it does
1. Loads and checks the registration data.
2. Finds trends: yearly growth, fuel types, electric share, states, busy months.
3. **ML 1, forecasting:** compares 3 methods on a 12-month test and forecasts the next 12 months.
4. **ML 2, clustering:** groups similar states with KMeans.
5. Saves 9 charts in `charts/`.

## Dataset
`data/registrations_synthetic.csv` has 23,250 rows: one for every month from Jan 2019 to Sep 2026,
state, vehicle type and fuel type.

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `date` | month (first day) |
| `state` | 10 states |
| `vehicle_class` | Two-Wheeler, Passenger Car, Three-Wheeler, Goods Vehicle, Bus |
| `fuel` | Petrol, Diesel, CNG, Electric, Hybrid |
| `registrations` | number of new registrations in that month |

The made-up data has yearly growth, festival-season peaks, a lockdown drop in 2020-21 and a
growing electric share.

**To use real data:** make a CSV with the same five columns and change the file name in Step 1 of
`vehicle_analysis.py`. For India, the government's Vahan dashboard is one place to look for real
data (check the site for what it lets you export).

## Method
- **Trend analysis:** pandas `groupby` and `pivot_table`, charts with matplotlib.
- **Forecasting:** only data from 2022 onwards is used because 2020-21 (lockdown) was not normal.
  The last 12 months are kept aside as a test. Three methods are compared:
  same as last year (baseline), Linear Regression, and Random Forest. Inputs are the month number
  and which month of the year it is. The best one (lowest average % error) forecasts the next 12 months.
- **Clustering:** KMeans on 3 numbers per state (growth, electric share, car share), after
  scaling. 2 and 3 groups were tried, and the one with the better silhouette score was kept.

## Results
Numbers below are from the **made-up** data.

### Trends
- Yearly registrations: 10.6M (2019), 7.9M (2020, **-25.6%** in the lockdown), 15.2M (2025).
  Growth keeps slowing down: +22.6% (2022), +11.6% (2023), +6.6% (2024), +4.7% (2025).
- Last 12 months: 15.66M registrations, **+4.2%** compared with the 12 months before.
- Lowest month: April 2020 with only about 37,500 registrations.
- Busiest months: October (+22.6% above an average month), March (+12.3%), November (+11.4%).
  Slowest: June (-11.9%) and April (-10.3%).
- Two-wheelers are 72% of registrations, cars 18%.
- Biggest states: Uttar Pradesh (2.72M), Maharashtra (2.43M), Tamil Nadu (1.93M).

![Busy and slow months](charts/6_month_pattern.png)

### Fuel types and electric vehicles
- Electric share of new vehicles: 0.6% (2019), 7.3% (2025), 9.5% (2026 so far).
- Diesel fell from 11.1% to 8.4% (2025). Petrol is still the biggest at 79.7% (2025).
- Electric share by vehicle type in 2026: three-wheelers 55%, buses 14%, two-wheelers 9%, cars 4%.

### Forecast
Test on the last 12 months (average % error):

| Method | Error |
|---|---|
| Random Forest | 3.1% |
| Same as last year | 4.0% |
| Linear Regression | 5.0% |

Random Forest was closest, so it was used for Oct 2026 to Sep 2027: about **15.8 million**
registrations, only +0.7% more than the last 12 months (see Limitations).

![Forecast test](charts/7_forecast_test.png)
![Forecast](charts/8_forecast.png)

### State groups
KMeans with 3 groups (silhouette score 0.36):
- **Group 1:** Gujarat, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra, Telangana, Tamil Nadu
- **Group 2:** Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal (lower EV share, fewer cars)
- **Group 3:** Delhi alone (highest EV share at 12%, most cars, almost no growth)

![State groups](charts/9_state_groups.png)

## Limitations
- The data is made up, so the findings are not about the real market.
- Random Forest cannot continue an upward trend, so its +0.7% forecast is probably too low if the
  recent +4% growth continues. Linear Regression went the other way and over-estimated.
- Only about 4 years of normal data are used and the test covers only one year, so the "best
  method" could change with more data.
- The state groups are weak (silhouette 0.36) and Delhi ended up as a group of one.
- The forecast ignores things like new taxes, new rules or supply problems.
